# 3. Results

## 3.1 Language shares

All tests are Type III tests with Satterthwaite denominator degrees of freedom. For English share (Figures 1 and 2), the main effect of lens language was significant in all eight models (F(2, 2376) = 2,245–31,047, all p < .001). Post-hoc comparisons showed that, within every prompt level, the English band was 0.02–0.58 larger under the English-fitted lens than under the Chinese-fitted lens (|t(2376)| = 3.2–147.4, all p < .005); the contrast with the Russian-fitted lens did not have a consistent sign across models and prompts (corrected p up to .50), and the Chinese-fitted and Russian-fitted lenses differed in some models only (corrected p up to .65). The main effect of prompt language was also significant in all eight models (F(2, 2376) = 2,986–23,348, all p < .001): the English band was largest at the English prompt, at 55.4% against 31.3% at the Russian prompt and 25.2% at the Chinese prompt under the English-fitted lens, and simple-effect tests of prompt language within every lens level were significant in all eight models (all p < .001). The prompt × lens interaction was significant in all eight models (F(4, 2376) = 22–1,414, all p < .001), so the size of the prompt effect differed across lenses.

For Chinese share, the same structure appeared with the bands reversed. Lens language was significant in all eight models (F(2, 2376) = 9,704–79,409, all p < .001), and post-hoc comparisons placed the Chinese-fitted lens above both other lenses, by 0.22–0.77 against the English-fitted lens (|t(2376)| = 55.6–222.7) and by 0.19–0.78 against the Russian-fitted lens (|t(2376)| = 58.5–211.4), all p < .001. Prompt language was significant in all eight models (F(2, 2376) = 4,687–29,857, all p < .001), and the Chinese band was largest at the Chinese prompt, at 66.2% against 47.3% at the Russian prompt and 44.4% at the English prompt under the Chinese-fitted lens. The prompt × lens interaction was significant in all eight models (F(4, 2376) = 21–1,339, all p < .001).

For Russian share, lens language was significant in all eight models (F(2, 2376) = 3,958–79,698, all p < .001), and the Russian-fitted lens exceeded the Chinese-fitted lens by 0.06–0.68 (|t(2376)| = 19.6–228.0) and the English-fitted lens by 0.05–0.72 (|t(2376)| = 17.5–243.0), all p < .001. Prompt language was significant in all eight models (F(2, 2376) = 1,652–39,916, all p < .001), and the Russian band was largest at the Russian prompt, at 39.8% against 26.8% at the English prompt and 19.5% at the Chinese prompt under the Russian-fitted lens. The prompt × lens interaction was significant in all eight models (F(4, 2376) = 163–2,626, all p < .001).

The remaining mass split between the other-language and the non-language band. Both bands varied with lens language and with prompt language in all eight models (all p < .001), and the other-language band stayed below 8% in every cell (2.6–7.6%). Probe family entered every model significantly (F(2, 297) = 193–1,062 for the English band, 34–998 for the Chinese band and 27–1,190 for the Russian band, all p < .001), and the three-way interaction was significant in all eight models for every band (F(8, 2376) = 32–1,293, all p < .001). English share was highest for the country-capital family (P1, 29.4%), intermediate for the category-clue family (P4, 26.4%) and lowest for the definitional family (P3, 20.9%), and the other-language band followed the same order (6.6%, 4.3% and 4.3%).

The composition results describe the structure of the candidate pool rather than the identity of the answer. Hit@100, the share of measured cells in which the answer family appeared among the top-100 decoded candidates, spanned 0.043–0.241 across the 72 model × lens × prompt-language combinations (mean 0.124; P4 0.154, P1 0.138, P3 0.082).

## 3.2 Semantic overlap

For three-way overlap (Figure 3), cross-language overlap without order was far above identical-string overlap without order at every depth: across the eight models, 41.2–45.5% of positions carried a translated counterpart in each of the other two lists (m1), against 11.1–12.6% carrying the same string (m3), a ratio of about 3.7.

Ordering agreements were rarer. Requiring the same rank reduced cross-language overlap to 8.2–12.9% (m2) and identical-string overlap to about 0.1% (m4), so the rank-preserving fraction was about a quarter for translation matches (m2/m1 = 0.25) and about 0.7% for string matches (m4/m3).

Prompt language moved the overlap in all eight models (m1: F(2, 594) = 6.8–1,761; m2: F(2, 594) = 47–3,232; m3: F(2, 594) = 85–642; all p ≤ .0012). Cross-language overlap without order was highest under English (45.5%) and Chinese (44.3%) prompts and lowest under Russian prompts (41.2%), and the ordered cross-language measure followed the same order (11.7%, 12.9% and 8.2%). Identical-string overlap was highest under Chinese prompts (12.6% against 11.3% and 11.1%), and the prompt contrasts were significant in every model (|t(594)| = 7.2–27.1 against the English prompt and 4.7–35.1 against the Russian prompt, all p < .001).

Probe family moved the overlap as well (m1: F(2, 297) = 10.5–846; m3: F(2, 297) = 27–1,455; all p < .001), and the family × prompt interaction was significant in all eight models (F(4, 594) = 105–678 for m1 and 90–1,278 for m3, all p < .001). The country-capital family gave the highest cross-language and identical-string overlap (m1 50.9%, m3 15.8%), the category-clue family the lowest cross-language overlap (38.9%) and the definitional family the lowest identical-string overlap (8.9%).

The ordered identical-string measure sat at the floor in every cell (about 0.1%). Its prompt effect was significant (F(2, 594–891) = 5.1–78.5, p ≤ .006), but its family effect was not in every model (F(2, 297) = 0.8–149.5, p up to .43), and three of the eight fits were singular, so m4 is reported as a descriptive bound rather than as a tested quantity.

Read together, the measures show that what the lenses share is the translated concept rather than the literal token: agreement between languages was about 3.7 times as frequent as agreement on the same string, and even when rank was required the cross-language measure exceeded the identical-string one by about two orders of magnitude.

## 3.3 Blackout rates

For pool blackout (Figure 4), lens language was the strongest factor in every model (F(2, 2376) = 199–10,610, all p < .001). Pools went dark most often under the English-fitted lens (0.228, 0.241 and 0.235 under English, Chinese and Russian prompts), less often under the Russian-fitted lens (0.169, 0.185 and 0.194) and least often under the Chinese-fitted lens (0.085, 0.019 and 0.070). Prompt language was significant in all eight models (F(2, 2376) = 125–564, all p < .001), as were the prompt × lens interaction (F(4, 2376) = 51–429), the probe-family effect (F(2, 297) = 40–451) and every higher-order term (F(8, 2376) ≥ 41), all p < .001. Post-hoc comparisons put the English-fitted lens above the Chinese-fitted lens (|t(2376)| = 19.9–114.5) and the Russian-fitted lens above the Chinese-fitted lens (|t(2376)| = 3.5–135.3) in every model (all p < .002).

The same ordering held when the pool was not wholly dark (Figure 4). On layers where exactly one lens was dark and the other two read, the dark lens was the English-fitted one in 0.101–0.122 of layers, the Russian-fitted one in 0.061–0.067 and the Chinese-fitted one in 0.004–0.016. Lens language (F(2, 2376–2673) = 115–7,861), prompt language (F(2, 2376–2673) = 17–530) and their interaction (F(4, 2376–2673) = 26–688) were significant in all eight models (all p < .001), as was the three-way interaction (F(8, 2376–2673) = 41–283).

The mirror image appeared for survival (Figure 4). On layers where exactly one lens read and the other two were dark, the survivor was the Chinese-fitted lens in 0.063–0.106 of layers, the Russian-fitted lens in 0.003–0.024 and the English-fitted lens in 0.002–0.008. Lens language (F(2, 2376) = 8.8–7,335), prompt language (F(2, 2376) = 6.2–488) and the prompt × lens interaction (F(4, 2376) = 34–657) were significant in all eight models (p ≤ .0021), and so was the three-way interaction (F(8, 2376) = 27–626). Post-hoc comparisons placed the Chinese-fitted lens above the English-fitted lens in every model (|t(2376)| = 4.0–103.7, all p < .001).

Across the three outcomes the failure was one-sided: the English-fitted lens went dark and rarely survived alone, while the Chinese-fitted lens kept reading and rarely failed alone. Because the prompt × lens interaction was significant in every model and the rates moved with the prompt language, the asymmetry belongs to the pairing between a lens and the prompt language rather than to a fixed lens.
